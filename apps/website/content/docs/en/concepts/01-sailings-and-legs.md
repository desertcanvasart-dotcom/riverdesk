# Routes, itineraries, sailings and legs

A Nile vessel sails a fixed loop, and guests join and leave it at different ports. Riverdesk describes that with four ideas.

## The weekly route (template)

The stops the vessel makes in order, with the day of each. For example, a 7-day loop:

| Day | Stop                              |
| --- | --------------------------------- |
| 0   | Luxor → Esna                      |
| 1   | Edfu                              |
| 2   | Kom Ombo                          |
| 4   | Aswan                             |
| 5   | Kom Ombo, Edfu                    |
| 6   | Esna                              |
| 7   | Luxor (and the loop starts again) |

## Itineraries (sailing patterns)

What you sell on that route: where guests board and where they leave. For example:

- **LXR-4**: 4 nights from Luxor to Aswan;
- **ASW-3**: 3 nights from Aswan to Luxor;
- **LXR-7** and **ASW-7**: the full loop from either end.

## Legs (departures)

A **leg** is the vessel sailing from one boarding port to the next, for example Luxor → Aswan on Monday 2 November. On the 7-day loop above there are two legs a week: Luxor → Aswan and Aswan → Luxor.

Legs are what cabins are counted on. Inventory, manifests and cabin numbers are all per leg.

## Sailings

A **sailing** is one itinerary on one date: _LXR-7 on 2 November_. It uses one or more legs. LXR-7 on 2 November uses both legs of that week, while LXR-4 on the same day uses only the first.

This is why one cabin can be sold to a 4-night guest from Luxor and then to a 3-night guest from Aswan the same week, but never to two guests on the same leg.

## Exceptions

Real life changes the plan: low water at Esna, a port closure, dry dock. Schedule changes are made on legs (cancel, move dates, change port) and Riverdesk recomputes every sailing that uses them. See [Schedule](/docs/staff-guide/schedule).
